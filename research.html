  • Violation of Bell's Inequality with Entangled Photon Pairs

    Bell's inequality tells us before being measured, some particles must have defined variables. Finding a violation of this inequality therefore means particles exist that, prior to being measured, do not inhibit any defined or 'hidden' variables, and are therefore inherently stochastic. This paper explores this observation by repeating a famous experiment.

  • Analyzing Higher-Order Gravitational Interactions with Pertubation Theory

    Gravitational interactions in celestial bodies are difficult to analyze, and higher order ones are often impossible to find analytically. However, using pertubation theory, these higher-order interactions can be approximated and analyzed to give a better understanding of the equations of motion between celestial bodies.

  • Morin Transition Temperature of α-Hematite using Mössbauer Spectroscopy

    The Morin temperature is a value that determines whether hematite acts ferromagnetically or antiferromagnetically. This paper analyzes what that temperature is for α-hematite, a special form of hematite, using a method known as Mössbauer Spectroscopy. This experiment is a repeat of one that was used to determine the water content on Mars in 2010, carried out by NASA.

    • Optics
    • Chemistry

  • Effects of Magnetic Disturbances on the Phenom Pro Scanning Electron Microscope using Applied Magnetic Fields

    A scanning electron microscope uses magnetic fields to focus electrons onto a sample. By introducing external magnetic fields, this can disturb the way the focusing acts, causing detrimental effects on the resulting images. This paper quantitatively looks at the way the image can be altered using these applied external fields.
